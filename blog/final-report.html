GSoC 2026 Final Report — Tableau Infrastructure and Multirate Methods

August 7, 202610 min readGSoC

This is my final work product for Google Summer of Code 2026 with SciML (NumFOCUS), mentored by Chris Rackauckas. The proposal had two deliverables for OrdinaryDiffEq.jl: migrate solver families from hand-written per-method implementations to generic tableau-driven forms, and build a family of multirate integrators for split ODE systems, finishing with public benchmarks. Both shipped. Over the project I merged 68 pull requests into OrdinaryDiffEq.jl, 7 into SciMLBenchmarks.jl, 3 into SciMLOperators.jl, and 1 into NonlinearSolve.jl, with 5 more under review as I write this.

I want to thank Chris for the fast reviews and for holding every PR to the same standard, which taught me more than the code did.

Multirate methods

OrdinaryDiffEq.jl had no multirate solvers when the project started. It now has a registered subpackage, OrdinaryDiffEqMultirate, containing ten methods, and a pending restructuring that splits the implicit members into OrdinaryDiffEqMultirateImplicit so the explicit package stays dependency-light.

MREIL factorizes W once per extrapolation column and reuses it across substeps, reuses the Jacobian across rejected steps, and was verified as a W-method whose Richardson order survives a frozen Jacobian. In the benchmark's favorable regime the multirate methods run about an order of magnitude faster at loose tolerances and 2x to 6x at tight ones; the null case shows where single-rate methods win, so the diagrams show where multirate stops paying and not only where it wins. Writing the benchmark also surfaced that the explicit MRI-GARK error estimators cannot see micro instability, documented in the notebook, and a solver crash filed as #4149.

Current state and what is left

Everything above is merged and released except the five open PRs, which are under review. Remaining future work: register OrdinaryDiffEqMultirateImplicit and add MREIL to the benchmark notebook once released, and the MrGARK family of Roberts, Loffeld, Sarshar, Woodward and Sandu (issue #961), which is scoped but not started.

What I learned

The recurring lesson was that a fix is not done when the tests pass: three times a test I wrote also passed on the unfixed code, and mutation testing, deleting the fix and checking the suite goes red, became my default. The other lesson was to measure before claiming. Several times a plausible explanation of a bug did not survive an A/B run, and the runs were always cheaper than being wrong in review.

Future prospects

I plan to stay involved with SciML past the program: the open PRs through review, the MrGARK family, and stochastic Taylor and RODE-Taylor integration as the next arc.
