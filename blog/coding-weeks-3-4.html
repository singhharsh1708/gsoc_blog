GSoC 2026 Weeks 3–4 — IMEX-RK Methods & the First Multirate Solvers

June 13, 20267 min readGSoC

With the generic tableau hardened in Weeks 1–2, this fortnight was about leverage: finishing the IMEX-RK family on the shared stepper, then pivoting to the part of the project I'm most excited about — the first multirate solvers in OrdinaryDiffEq.jl.

Completing the IMEX-RK family

The ARS methods landed at the close of the last cycle. This fortnight rounded out the family, each addition a tableau over the generic kernel rather than a bespoke stepper.

  • #3705: Added the IMEX-SSP family (strong-stability-preserving) and extended ESDIRKIMEXTableau to support non-ESDIRK structures.
  • #3706: Added the BHR(5,5,3) IMEX-RK method (Boscarino–Russo 2009).
Stretching the abstraction

The IMEX-SSP methods don't share the explicit-first-stage assumption baked into the original ESDIRK-shaped tableau. #3705 had to generalize the abstraction to admit non-ESDIRK structures without regressing the ESDIRK fast path — a good test of whether the refactor was genuinely general or just ESDIRK-in-disguise.

The first multirate solvers

Multirate methods exploit the fact that in many systems some components evolve far faster than others. Instead of forcing the whole system onto the smallest stable step, you integrate the fast and slow partitions with different step sizes and couple them carefully. This is the core deliverable of my proposal, and the first solvers landed this fortnight.

  • #3707: Added the MRAB (multirate Adams–Bashforth, k-step) explicit solver.
  • #3717: Added MIS2 (Wensch–Knoth–Galant 2009) and refactored the multirate tableau/file structure so subsequent methods slot in cleanly.
  • #3724: Added the MRI-GARK-ERK22a and ERK22b methods (Sandu 2019) — my entry into the Multirate Infinitesimal GARK framework.
  • #3741: Fused the MRI-GARK slow-forcing accumulation to cut redundant work in the coupling step (follow-up to #3724).
The hardest debugging of the cycle

Translating Sandu's MRI-GARK coupling coefficients into an allocation-free stepper was straightforward; getting the slow-forcing accumulation to match the reference order numerically was not. A convergence study that plateaus at order 1.8 instead of 2.0 means a coupling term is subtly misplaced — and the only way to find it is to bisect the accumulation against a hand-worked reference step.

Performance win

The follow-up #3741 fused the slow-forcing accumulation so the coupling contribution is built up in a single pass instead of re-summing per inner step — less redundant work in the hottest loop of the multirate stepper, with the numerics unchanged.

In flight

As I write this, the MRI-GARK order ladder is still climbing, plus a cross-repo fix:

  • #3747 (open): Adds the 3rd-order MRI-GARK-ERK33a and unifies the MRI-GARK formulation so all orders share one path.
  • #3751 (open): Adds the 4th-order explicit MRI-GARK-ERK45a.
  • MTK #4609 (open): A cross-repo fix in ModelingToolkit.jl — forwarding the outer ODEFunction's flag down to the inner nonlinear problem in ODENLStepData.

There was also some routine upkeep: runic formatting for the RKN steppers (#3711) and a tolerance bump on the autonomous Robertson MTK test after the nlstep path changed (#3716).

Challenges this cycle

  • Non-ESDIRK structures: as noted above, the IMEX-SSP methods forced the tableau abstraction to generalize beyond its original ESDIRK assumptions.
  • File-structure churn: #3717's refactor of the multirate tableau/file layout was a deliberate investment — paying a one-time reorganization cost so #3724, #3747, and #3751 could each be small, focused additions.

Goals for the next two weeks

Heading toward the midterm checkpoint:

  • Land #3747 and #3751 to complete the explicit MRI-GARK order ladder (2nd → 4th).
  • Start the LowStorageRK migration in earnest — the 2N-form scaffold is staged and needs the 2C/3S*/3S*+ variants built on top.
  • Push toward an implicit/IMEX MRI-GARK variant now that the explicit framework is unified.
  • Keep the convergence and zero-allocation suites green across everything added.

Closing thoughts

This is the stretch where the architecture stopped being a promise and started being a tool. Completing the IMEX family was almost mechanical thanks to the Weeks 1–2 groundwork — but multirate is genuinely new ground, and getting the first MRI-GARK methods to match their reference orders has been the most satisfying part of GSoC so far. Onward to the midterm.
