GSoC 2026 Weeks 9–10 — Completing the Implicit Multirate Family & a Wrong-Answer Bug Hunt

July 26, 202618 min readGSoC

Last cycle ended with two implicit MRI-GARK methods in the package and a promise to push the order ladder higher. The fourth-order member that completes the family went up the evening before this fortnight began and spent the whole of it in review — and writing it was what exposed the fact that the machinery the two previous methods were sitting on had been ignoring the tableau embedding entirely. That set the tone for the rest of the cycle, which turned into a hunt for bugs that don't crash. They return the wrong answer, confidently, with ReturnCode.Success attached.

Completing the implicit MRI-GARK family

With #3886 in review, the implicit order ladder is written up to 2 → 3 → 4, matching the explicit family I closed out in Weeks 5–6; it lands when the PR does.

  • #3886: Adds MRI-GARK-ESDIRK46a (Sandu 2019, order 4), completing the implicit MRI-GARK family alongside IRK21a (#3811) and ESDIRK34a (#3812), and marked as closing the long-standing issue #961. It also replaces the repeated Union{MRIGARKIRK21a, MRIGARKESDIRK34a} spellings with the MRIGARKImplicitAlg alias that already existed for exactly this purpose. Validation was more involved than the coefficients suggest: fourth-order convergence in-place and out-of-place, the embedded error estimator checked across tolerances, the existing IRK21a/ESDIRK34a orders confirmed unchanged, and the multirate suite at 99/99.
The new method that found the old bug

The implicit MRI-GARK path never used the tableau embedding. It always estimated the local error as u - z[s], the difference between the step result and the last stage value. For ESDIRK46a, which is stiffly accurate, those two quantities are the same thing — so the error estimate came out identically zero and the controller accepted every single step regardless of what the solution was doing. The fix uses the embedding row when the tableau has one, matching the explicit implementation, and preserves the old behaviour for methods without an embedding.

A cluster of wrong-answer bugs in NonlinearSolveAlg

With the multirate work handed off to review, most of the rest of the fortnight went into NonlinearSolveAlg — the layer that lets an implicit ODE solver delegate its stage solve to any algorithm from NonlinearSolve.jl. Two of the three bugs I found there share an uncomfortable property: nothing throws, nothing warns, and the solve reports success. The third crashes outright — but only for a class of inner solver nobody had pointed at this path before.

  • #3893: Gates first-iteration convergence on the inner solver's state, fixing issue #3817. A globalized solver like TrustRegion can reject its trial step: step! returns with the iterate unmoved and the cache not terminated, having only shrunk its trust region. The outer Newton loop measures displacement, so that reads as ndz == 0 — which both convergence tests call a perfect solve, and the stage is accepted with no correction applied. On fixed-dt FBDF + TrustRegion against Robertson, master returned Success with the state frozen at u0.
  • #3900: (closed, not merged) Under W-reuse, #3885 handed the reused ODE W to the inner NonlinearFunction as jac_prototype = MatrixOperator(W) with no jac, making the inner Jacobian an operator. NewtonRaphson only ever needs J \ f, which an operator supplies happily. TrustRegion builds its step from a concrete Jacobian, so given an operator it works from a model that doesn't match the residual, mismanages the trust radius, and converges to the wrong answer while the outer solve sees a small displacement and accepts the stage — reproduced with FBDF on Robertson with an analytic jac. The PR was closed rather than merged; the diagnosis is the part worth keeping.
  • #3959: Fixes NonlinearSolveAlg crashing and stalling with polyalgorithm inner solvers, addressing the FieldError in issue #3859. NonlinearSolvePolyAlgorithmCache — what RobustMultiNewton and FastShortcutNonlinearPolyalg hand back — keeps u and fu on its active subcache, not at top level, so every raw cache.cache.u-style read in newton.jl threw the moment a polyalgorithm was used as the inner solver. Its reinit! also left force_stop and retcode at their converged values, so a step!-driven loop silently no-ops forever after and reuses a stale iterate.

When that predicate fires, nlsolve! skips convergence and divergence bookkeeping for the iteration — a zero ndz would also poison the next iteration's theta — and lets the inner solver retry with its shrunken region. If the iterate never moves, the loop exhausts and the step is rejected as unconverged, which is what should have happened all along.

Why a wrong answer is worse than a crash

A MethodError is a gift: it names a file, a line, and a type, and nobody ships a paper on the back of it. #3893 and #3900 give you the opposite — a number of the right shape and magnitude, with a Success retcode, that is simply not the solution to the problem you posed. Neither had a failing test to work backwards from, because from the test suite's point of view nothing was failing. Both had to be found by asking a structural question instead: what does this convergence test assume about the inner solver, and which inner solvers violate it?

#3959 is worth one more note. My first instinct was to teach newton.jl how to reach into a polyalgorithm's active subcache — which would have worked, and would have been a workaround living permanently in the wrong package. Per review, both halves belong at the interface level upstream, so the accessors and the reinit! reset went to NonlinearSolve.jl #1115 (opened today), and #3959 is being reworked into the consumer side only — every polyalgorithm-unsafe inner-cache read in newton.jl routed through the upstream get_u/get_fu accessors, with no local workaround left behind.

The operator mass-matrix chain, continued

The thread I picked up in Weeks 7–8 — code that quietly assumes the mass matrix is a concrete Matrix — kept producing hits as I pushed further along the same path.

  • #3916: Fixes a DimensionMismatch in jacobian2W! for a ScalarOperator mass matrix (issue #3915). Both jacobian2W! and jacobian2W special-case mass_matrix isa UniformScaling to skip the axes(mass_matrix) == axes(W) boundscheck. A ScalarOperator is the same object mathematically — λ·I — but isn't a UniformScaling and reports axes(mm) == (), so it fell into the boundscheck meant for full matrices. This is the next link in the chain #3814 → SciMLOperators#400 → #3837 → #3916: the first place a ScalarOperator mass matrix actually reaches now that the entry crash and get_differential_vars are both already fixed.
  • #3923: Makes get_differential_vars check Diagonal.diag instead of the full matrix. It ran all(!iszero, mm) before reaching its _isdiag branch — for a Diagonal that walks all n² positions, turning every diagonal hit into a scalar getindex on the underlying vector. The added Diagonal dispatch broadcasts over mm.diag instead: O(n) for a question the type answers exactly, and no scalar getindex, which a device-resident vector disallows by default. Same reduction find_algebraic_vars_eqs(::Diagonal) uses a few lines away in the same file.
  • #3944: Unwraps operator mass matrices before findall in OrdinaryDiffEqDifferentiation. Several places seed a sparsity pattern with idxs = findall(!iszero, f.mass_matrix) and then index it — both of which need a concrete matrix, so pairing a SciMLOperator mass matrix with a jac_prototype threw MethodError: no method matching keys(::MatrixOperator{...}). One concrete_mass_matrix helper at all four sites removes the crash sites on the path reported in issue #2929.

A method that was never FSAL, and some housekeeping

  • #3975: Fixes ExplicitTaylor's fsallast aliasing to the state buffer. ExplicitTaylor and ExplicitTaylorAdaptiveOrder build each step from the Taylor jet and never reuse the previous step's last derivative, so they are not FSAL methods at all — but neither declared isfsal, so both silently inherited the true default. The fix declares them non-FSAL, after which the initial-step heuristic uses its own zero(u0) and the caches can return (nothing, nothing) exactly as FunctionMapCache already does.
  • #3945: Drops stale BaseThreads and Sequential imports from OrdinaryDiffEqExtrapolation, whose no_stale_explicit_imports QA check was failing on master; neither name is referenced anywhere in the package, while PolyesterThreads on the same import line genuinely is and stays. Small, but notable for a different reason: it is the only PR I both opened and merged inside this fortnight. Of the rest of the work in this post, three PRs were closed without merging and the remainder are still in review as I write.
  • #3897: (closed) Four @test_broken order assertions in modelingtoolkit/nlstep_tests.jl started passing, so the MTK lane reported Unexpected Pass and errored out. All four were NonlinearSolveAlg(TrustRegion()) convergence-order checks on TRBDF2 and QNDF2, and they were the failures behind #3807 and #3818 — since fixed by recent NonlinearSolve releases, SciML/NonlinearSolve.jl#1010 recomputing the trust-region radius on reinit!. The assertions had already been unbroken on the maintainer's side, so this closed within minutes of opening.
  • #3946: (closed) An attempt to restore OrdinaryDiffEqNonlinearSolve's solver-author public API. #3832 kept only the algorithm types and dropped the extension surface — build_nlsolver, nlsolve!, compute_step! and friends — which every downstream sublibrary implementing an implicit method still imports directly.
A first step of 1.0e-323

The inherited isfsal default made ode_determine_initdt write an extra f evaluation into integrator.fsallast — and since get_fsalfirstlast returned (cache.u, cache.u) with cache.u === integrator.u, that call was literally f(u, u, p, t), corrupting the state mid-evaluation for any in-place RHS that reads u after writing du. That is a completely normal way to write an RHS. With u0 = [1.0, 2.0], integrator.u came back from init as [-16.0, 16.0]; the step estimate saw a NaN, warned, and fell back to tdir * dtmin. On prob_ode_pleiades at abstol = reltol = 1e-10 that is a first step of 1.0e-323, and the controller spends hundreds of steps climbing back out — all before a single real step has been taken.

Challenges this cycle

  • A correct diagnosis is not a landed fix: #3900 took real work to reproduce and pin down, and it closed without merging. The wrong-answer behaviour was genuine and the analysis holds up, but a confirmed bug plus a working reproduction still does not guarantee that the patch you wrote is the one the package ends up wanting.
  • Working against a moving upstream: #3959 only makes sense once the polyalgorithm accessors exist in NonlinearSolveBase, so the consumer-side PR has to wait on a release of a package it doesn't control. Splitting a fix across two repositories is clearly the right call, but it turns one review into a dependency chain.
  • A long review queue: ten PRs opened inside this fortnight, one of them merged, three closed without merging, and seven — counting #3886, opened the evening before the window — still in the queue. That is a normal shape for two weeks in a package this size, but it does mean this post describes a lot of work whose final state I can't yet report.

Goals for the next two weeks

  • Get the review backlog through — #3886, #3893, #3916, #3923, #3944, #3959 and #3975 are all sitting in the queue, and landing them matters more than opening anything new.
  • Land the upstream half of the polyalgorithm fix in NonlinearSolve.jl, and get a release cut so #3959 has a compat floor to point at.
  • Sweep SciMLOperators.jl itself for the operator gaps my mass-matrix work keeps bumping into, rather than continuing to patch each consumer.
  • Keep pushing on the remaining polyalgorithm-as-inner-solver gaps — the NonlinearSolveAlg path has a fix in flight, but the same cache assumption almost certainly exists elsewhere in the package.

Closing thoughts

The headline of this fortnight is that the implicit multirate deliverable is written end to end and sitting in review — IRK21a, ESDIRK34a and now ESDIRK46a, an order ladder to match the explicit one. But the part I'll remember is what writing the last rung turned up. A stiffly accurate method is the one case where u - z[s] is exactly zero, so adding ESDIRK46a is precisely what it took to notice that the implicit path had never used its embedding: a zero error estimate, and beside it a state frozen at u0 and a converged wrong root. None of the three announced themselves. Each one had to be reasoned into existence before it could be fixed — and most of that reasoning is still sitting in the queue as I write, so the next update will have to report on where it all landed.
